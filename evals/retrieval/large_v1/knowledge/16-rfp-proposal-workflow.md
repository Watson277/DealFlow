# DealFlow RFP and Proposal Workflow Governance

Document status: current
Effective from: 2025-07-01
Product version: 3.2

## Proposal Workflow Scope and Eligibility

This business area brings together RFP Intake, Requirement Extraction, Capability Assessment. The grouping follows operational ownership and reader workflow; each subsection retains its own plan, version, status, limits, exceptions, and evidence.

| Topic | Capability state | Commercial scope |
|---|---|---|
| RFP Intake | Enterprise-only capability | Business |
| Requirement Extraction | Dedicated-only capability | Enterprise |
| Capability Assessment | standard capability | Dedicated |

### RFP Intake

For rfp intake, version 3.2 classifies the function as a Enterprise-only capability for Business. Its control record is reviewed every 45 days, operational evidence is sampled every 6 hours, and any approved exception expires after 35 days.

Applicability and contract treatment for rfp intake. The controlling dimensions are the contracted edition, deployment model, data region, effective version, and recorded capability state. The catalog classification is Enterprise-only capability, with the commercial scope recorded as Business. A sales response may narrow that scope for a customer, but it may not silently broaden it. If an order form and this guide disagree, operations pauses activation and asks the regional operations lead to resolve the discrepancy in writing.

Configuration workflow for rfp intake. Security Assurance opens the governing record before the setting is enabled, records the tenant and subscription, selects the authorized region, and links the approval. A second operator validates the resulting control in Singapore; production use begins only after the tenant control report shows the expected value and a successful timestamp. The configuration is rechecked every 45 days and after any edition, identity-provider, network, or residency change that could alter eligibility.

Exception handling for rfp intake. An exception must identify the precise unmet condition, the compensating control, an accountable owner, and an expiry no later than 35 days after approval. Renewal is a new decision rather than an automatic extension. A roadmap statement cannot serve as a compensating control, and a professional-services estimate does not prove that a capability is active. When the exception expires, the system either restores the documented baseline or disables the dependent workflow.

Operational verification for rfp intake. The service samples the active setting every 6 hours while a change is open and compares it with the tenant registry. A mismatch creates a case for Security Assurance, preserves the previous and proposed values, and blocks a compliance export from showing the control as passed. The result does not certify adjacent identity, logging, resilience, or integration controls that happen to use a similar term.

Evidence and auditability for rfp intake. The customer package contains the tenant control report, effective version, UTC activation time, approving role, last verification result, and any open exception. Tenant secrets, personal data, and raw customer content are redacted, but the plan, region, status, threshold, and unit remain visible. A screenshot without tenant and version context is supporting material only. Reviewers can determine whether the rule was current, historical, planned, custom, or unavailable at the time.

Failure and recovery for rfp intake. If verification fails, new dependent operations stop while already committed records remain readable. The owner triages whether the cause is configuration drift, an expired entitlement, a regional restriction, or a version mismatch. Customer-impacting failures use the contracted support channel, and restoration requires a clean verification run plus approval from the regional operations lead. Recovery does not reset a separate SLA, RTO, RPO, log-retention, or data-residency clock.

Customer responsibilities for rfp intake. The customer supplies accurate tenant identifiers, maintains authorized contacts, and reports subscription or regional changes before relying on the control in production. Where the customer operates an external identity provider, key service, network appliance, integration platform, or on-premises component, its availability remains a customer dependency. DealFlow enforces the documented hosted boundary and produces the tenant control report for the portion it operates.

A conforming procurement example is a tenant whose order form matches Business, whose control is activated in Singapore, and whose evidence package identifies the current version. The assessor accepts the result only for rfp intake; the same package cannot be reused to claim a different capability. If the customer later moves to mainland China, the old verification remains historical evidence and a new regional check is required.

A non-conforming example is an implementation team citing a similarly named policy with a different number, lifecycle state, or subscription. The adjacent proposal workflow control uses a 120-day window and applies to Enterprise; those values are not interchangeable. The discrepancy is material even when both statements appear in official DealFlow documents, because the older or neighboring statement answers a different business condition. The response is corrected before proposal approval and the rejected interpretation remains in the audit trail.

Change management for rfp intake. Material changes to eligibility, numeric limits, region coverage, or capability status are announced at least 365 days before they take effect unless an urgent security correction requires a shorter window. The notice names the old value, new value, affected subscriptions, migration action, and authoritative replacement document. Existing exceptions retain their original expiry but are reassessed against the new baseline. Historical text stays available for audit purposes and is marked superseded.

For workflow permissions, version 3.2 classifies the function as a standard capability for Enterprise and Dedicated. Its control record is reviewed every 45 days, operational evidence is sampled every 12 hours, and any approved exception expires after 35 days.

Applicability and contract treatment for workflow permissions. The controlling dimensions are the contracted edition, deployment model, data region, effective version, and recorded capability state. The catalog classification is standard capability, with the commercial scope recorded as Enterprise and Dedicated. A sales response may narrow that scope for a customer, but it may not silently broaden it. If an order form and this guide disagree, operations pauses activation and asks the change advisory board to resolve the discrepancy in writing.

Configuration workflow for workflow permissions. Regional Reliability opens the governing record before the setting is enabled, records the tenant and subscription, selects the authorized region, and links the approval. A second operator validates the resulting control in Sydney; production use begins only after the UTC-stamped change ticket shows the expected value and a successful timestamp. The configuration is rechecked every 45 days and after any edition, identity-provider, network, or residency change that could alter eligibility.

Exception handling for workflow permissions. An exception must identify the precise unmet condition, the compensating control, an accountable owner, and an expiry no later than 35 days after approval. Renewal is a new decision rather than an automatic extension. A roadmap statement cannot serve as a compensating control, and a professional-services estimate does not prove that a capability is active. When the exception expires, the system either restores the documented baseline or disables the dependent workflow.

Operational verification for workflow permissions. The service samples the active setting every 12 hours while a change is open and compares it with the tenant registry. A mismatch creates a case for Regional Reliability, preserves the previous and proposed values, and blocks a compliance export from showing the control as passed. The result does not certify adjacent identity, logging, resilience, or integration controls that happen to use a similar term.

Evidence and auditability for workflow permissions. The customer package contains the UTC-stamped change ticket, effective version, UTC activation time, approving role, last verification result, and any open exception. Tenant secrets, personal data, and raw customer content are redacted, but the plan, region, status, threshold, and unit remain visible. A screenshot without tenant and version context is supporting material only. Reviewers can determine whether the rule was current, historical, planned, custom, or unavailable at the time.

Failure and recovery for workflow permissions. If verification fails, new dependent operations stop while already committed records remain readable. The owner triages whether the cause is configuration drift, an expired entitlement, a regional restriction, or a version mismatch. Customer-impacting failures use the contracted support channel, and restoration requires a clean verification run plus approval from the change advisory board. Recovery does not reset a separate SLA, RTO, RPO, log-retention, or data-residency clock.

Customer responsibilities for workflow permissions. The customer supplies accurate tenant identifiers, maintains authorized contacts, and reports subscription or regional changes before relying on the control in production. Where the customer operates an external identity provider, key service, network appliance, integration platform, or on-premises component, its availability remains a customer dependency.
