# DealFlow Support Plans and Incident Response Policy

Document status: current
Effective from: 2025-07-01
Product version: 3.2

## Support Operations Scope and Eligibility

This business area brings together Business Support Hours, Enterprise Support Hours, Dedicated Support Coverage. The grouping follows operational ownership and reader workflow; each subsection retains its own plan, version, status, limits, exceptions, and evidence.

| Topic | Capability state | Commercial scope |
|---|---|---|
| Business Support Hours | standard capability | Business |
| Enterprise Support Hours | Enterprise-only capability | Enterprise |
| Dedicated Support Coverage | Dedicated-only capability | Dedicated |

### Business Support Hours

For business support hours, version 3.2 classifies the function as a standard capability for Business. Its control record is reviewed every 90 days, operational evidence is sampled every 1 hours, and any approved exception expires after 35 days.

Applicability and contract treatment for business support hours. The controlling dimensions are the contracted edition, deployment model, data region, effective version, and recorded capability state. The catalog classification is standard capability, with the commercial scope recorded as Business. A sales response may narrow that scope for a customer, but it may not silently broaden it. If an order form and this guide disagree, operations pauses activation and asks the tenant security administrator to resolve the discrepancy in writing.

Configuration workflow for business support hours. Customer Success opens the governing record before the setting is enabled, records the tenant and subscription, selects the authorized region, and links the approval. A second operator validates the resulting control in Virginia; production use begins only after the regional assurance workbook shows the expected value and a successful timestamp. The configuration is rechecked every 90 days and after any edition, identity-provider, network, or residency change that could alter eligibility.

Exception handling for business support hours. An exception must identify the precise unmet condition, the compensating control, an accountable owner, and an expiry no later than 35 days after approval. Renewal is a new decision rather than an automatic extension. A roadmap statement cannot serve as a compensating control, and a professional-services estimate does not prove that a capability is active. When the exception expires, the system either restores the documented baseline or disables the dependent workflow.

Operational verification for business support hours. The service samples the active setting every 1 hours while a change is open and compares it with the tenant registry. A mismatch creates a case for Customer Success, preserves the previous and proposed values, and blocks a compliance export from showing the control as passed. The result does not certify adjacent identity, logging, resilience, or integration controls that happen to use a similar term.

Evidence and auditability for business support hours. The customer package contains the regional assurance workbook, effective version, UTC activation time, approving role, last verification result, and any open exception. Tenant secrets, personal data, and raw customer content are redacted, but the plan, region, status, threshold, and unit remain visible. A screenshot without tenant and version context is supporting material only. Reviewers can determine whether the rule was current, historical, planned, custom, or unavailable at the time.

Failure and recovery for business support hours. If verification fails, new dependent operations stop while already committed records remain readable. The owner triages whether the cause is configuration drift, an expired entitlement, a regional restriction, or a version mismatch. Customer-impacting failures use the contracted support channel, and restoration requires a clean verification run plus approval from the tenant security administrator. Recovery does not reset a separate SLA, RTO, RPO, log-retention, or data-residency clock.

Customer responsibilities for business support hours. The customer supplies accurate tenant identifiers, maintains authorized contacts, and reports subscription or regional changes before relying on the control in production. Where the customer operates an external identity provider, key service, network appliance, integration platform, or on-premises component, its availability remains a customer dependency. DealFlow enforces the documented hosted boundary and produces the regional assurance workbook for the portion it operates.

A conforming procurement example is a tenant whose order form matches Business, whose control is activated in Virginia, and whose evidence package identifies the current version. The assessor accepts the result only for business support hours; the same package cannot be reused to claim a different capability. If the customer later moves to Sydney, the old verification remains historical evidence and a new regional check is required.

A non-conforming example is an implementation team citing a similarly named policy with a different number, lifecycle state, or subscription. The adjacent support operations control uses a 365-day window and applies to Enterprise; those values are not interchangeable. The discrepancy is material even when both statements appear in official DealFlow documents, because the older or neighboring statement answers a different business condition. The response is corrected before proposal approval and the rejected interpretation remains in the audit trail.

Change management for business support hours. Material changes to eligibility, numeric limits, region coverage, or capability status are announced at least 14 days before they take effect unless an urgent security correction requires a shorter window. The notice names the old value, new value, affected subscriptions, migration action, and authoritative replacement document. Existing exceptions retain their original expiry but are reassessed against the new baseline. Historical text stays available for audit purposes and is marked superseded.

Decision example 1. A Sydney tenant asks whether business support hours can be represented as a current contractual capability after a subscription or configuration change. Product Operations checks the effective guide, confirms the capability state and applicable unit, and compares the request with the explicit exclusion in this subsection. If every prerequisite is met, the decision is valid for 120 days before routine reassessment; otherwise the request is recorded as unavailable, custom, or exception-bound rather than being rounded up to standard support. The customer-readable rationale links to the evidence artifact so a later reviewer can reproduce the decision without undocumented product knowledge.

Decision example 2. A mainland China tenant asks whether business support hours can be represented as a current contractual capability after a subscription or configuration change. Security Assurance checks the effective guide, confirms the capability state and applicable unit, and compares the request with the explicit exclusion in this subsection. If every prerequisite is met, the decision is valid for 180 days before routine reassessment; otherwise the request is recorded as unavailable, custom, or exception-bound rather than being rounded up to standard support. The customer-readable rationale links to the evidence artifact so a later reviewer can reproduce the decision without undocumented product knowledge.
